# Owais's voice

Use this profile when drafting or revising anything published under Owais's
name: blog posts, Substack essays, and video narration. It is drawn from the
blog at desertthunder.dev (2025–2026) and the Substack at
desertavenger.substack.com (2026). Owais's own edits outrank this file.

## Two registers

Owais writes in two registers. Pick the one that fits, and let them mix in
video narration.

**Blog and dev log.** First person, informal, and enthusiastic. Explains
what was built, what broke, and what was learned.

- "Why it's called X I don't know. It's basically untitled."
- "Pretty cool stuff."
- "one of the more absurd ways I've chosen to learn a new programming
  language."
- "I can never seem to keep my free space over 20gb."

**Substack essays on politics and culture.** Argumentative, sourced, and
openly left. Section headings, numbered footnotes, image captions, and the
occasional table or timeline.

- Identifies with the left and says so: "the loosely affiliated cohort of
  critics (to which I find myself) sometimes called the dirtbag left."
- States the opposing case fairly, then gives a verdict in the first person:
  "Newsom's defenders in the democratic party frame this as an honest
  engagement with a difficult policy question. I see it as a straightforward
  example of an establishment politician abandoning a marginalized group."
- Concedes what is true before pressing: "Those wins are real, but do not erase
  the record above so much as make it more confusing."
- Brings in theory by name and explains it in a sentence: Mark Fisher's
  capitalist realism, Baudrillard's simulacra, Hegel's dialectic as Caesar
  misuses it.
- Uses specific, checkable detail: bill numbers, vote counts, poll figures,
  named sources.
- Ends on a hard judgment, sometimes with profanity: "The dystopia isn't the
  radioactive shithole, but the fact that no one in the wasteland questions
  this reality."

## Traits to keep

- First person when giving a judgment. Owais does not hide behind "critics
  argue" when the view is his own.
- Parenthetical asides and small jokes: "(think computers that filled entire
  rooms)", "Toilets restore HP in Fallout."
- Plain admissions of uncertainty or ignorance: "I have no idea if iaWriter
  exports PDFs", "This is unconfirmed but it seems like...".
- Credits inspirations and sources by name.
- Defines a term right after using it, in ordinary words.
- Pop culture as evidence and as a joke, drawn from games, TV, comics, and
  baseball.
- Sardonic labels for public figures, used sparingly: "bonafied cartoon
  supervillains."
- Invites the reader in: "If you want something like this, please let me
  know!"

## Traits to drop

Some published prose drifts into a generic academic register that does not
sound like Owais. Do not reproduce it:

- Abstract openers such as "offers a rigorous critique of the intersections
  between..." or "In this essay, I want to dive into...". Open with the
  concrete thing instead: the scene, the bill, the panel.
- Stacked "not X, but Y" reframes. One per essay is plenty.
- Inflated summary words: "the ultimate expression of", "zenith",
  "insidious", "second-order insight".
- Scare quotes around ordinary phrases.

## Narration

Video narration is spoken by an ElevenLabs clone of Owais's voice.

- Write for the ear: shorter sentences than the Substack, one idea per
  sentence, and names repeated where a reader would see a footnote.
- Keep the first person and the asides. They carry the voice once the
  footnotes and headings are gone.
- Say sources aloud briefly ("as Mark Fisher put it") and put the full
  citation on screen.
- Avoid nested parentheticals, long lists, and abbreviations the voice clone
  may mispronounce. Spell out numbers and acronyms the way Owais would say
  them.
